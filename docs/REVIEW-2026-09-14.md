## The headline

> "It's just very different to — it's just like loosely based on the design as opposed to…" — 11:53
> "the agent seems to have done its own thing" — 04:27
> "I'd rather we match a bit more closely what the intention is here, and then I give feedback to the specific thing, as opposed to — this seems a bit off target" — 23:20
> "I would follow this UI/UX even if it's like super simplistic, and then we can polish" — 24:58

This is not a bug list. The build is a competent app that answers a *different brief*. The 2019 document describes a specific, spare, opinionated product; what exists is a generic time-tracker with the right nouns. The instruction is to re-converge on the design first and polish second.

**There is a concrete reason this happened, and it is worth naming.** The PDF was converted to `claude_code_package/` — and the converter only captured the *typed text layer*. From p. 36 onward the design carries **handwritten orange and red annotations**, and the extraction dropped every one of them. Those annotations are where the product actually lives: they define the Pomodoro timer, the scheduled-vs-tracked distinction, the forecast calendar, the "type and estimate are optional" rule, drag-to-reprioritise, direct manipulation of the day ribbon, and activity notes. `ZEN_ROUTINE_SPEC.md` — the document any agent would have read — contains none of it, and its "Questions for Further Design" section asks questions the annotations already answer. The build was faithful to the source it was given.

## Part 1 — What the design says that nobody has read

Transcribed from the page images, not the text layer.

### The home surface is one screen, not five tabs (pp. 42–45)
A week strip (`M T W T F S S`, seven vertical colour columns), a breakdown pie, and the goals list — all on one surface. Two icons top-right switch modes: a **calendar** ("tap here to switch to calendar view") and a **stopwatch** ("tap the stopwatch to switch to tracking mode"). Planning and Tracking are *modes of the same screen*, not destinations in a tab bar. The week runs **Monday → Sunday**.

### The routine editor is a timeline ribbon you tap (pp. 36, 41)
> "**Tap on an activity to edit**" — annotation on p. 36

The day is a single horizontal ribbon running 7am → 11pm, coloured by activity type, labels above each segment, edited by tapping the segment itself. There is no list of block cards anywhere in the design; the ribbon *is* the editor, and the same ribbon reappears unchanged at the bottom of every tracking screen (pp. 74–77). Today's Routine screen is a vertical list of block cards — a different interaction model for the same data.

### Goals are a text list, and activity type and estimate are optional (pp. 45–67)
A goal row is: `[done?] [name] [activity-type icon, "?" when unset] [estimate, defaults to 1hr]`. That is the whole thing.

> "**Activity Type and Estimation are optional** (this allows for using the app like a simple ToDo list if desired — i.e. no scheduling functionality)"

> "Use the filter box to just see a single activity type"

> "**Adding Goals in filtered lists automatically gives the goal the activity type of the filter**"

> "**Drag and move rows around to reprioritise**"

Priority is *ordinal position in the list*. There is no priority field, no priority chips, no priority column.

> "we're moving things up and down — just grabbing a goal and moving it higher up the list or lower. And that changes the priority. We're not even showing the priority here; there's no column for priority, we're just ordering goals." — 26:17

### The forecast calendar (pp. 68–69) — the missing centrepiece
> "The calendar view combines data from your goals and your currently active routine, to show you **when you are forecasted to have completed your goals**. It gives you at-a-glance insight into what the future holds and the ability to re-prioritize goals or change your routine so you can hit your targets."

> "Much like the goals view, here too you can apply filters so you're only seeing say when your Health or Household goals are due to complete. You can also **change the granularity** so you're seeing just goal completion or % milestones."

The mock is a month grid with a dot per forecast completion, coloured by activity type, and a `50%` milestone badge on a long goal.

He then specified the algorithm out loud, which the design only implies:

> "when we have work blocks I should first be seeing the first one — Design first-time user experience flow, six hours — and then Integrate Analytics Framework. So let's say every day we have just a four-hour work block. Then I would see on Monday, Design FTUE flow; then on Tuesday the first two hours would be Design FTUE flow and **that goal would be done halfway through Tuesday**, and then we have two hours of Integrate Analytics Framework on Tuesday, and then that leaves six hours which don't fit, so we have four hours of Integrate Analytics on Wednesday, and then Thursday we would see Integrate Analytics Framework complete." — 27:18

That is a **fill-forward scheduler**: walk the routine forward day by day, pour each activity type's block minutes into that type's goals in priority order, and mark the day each goal's estimate is exhausted. It needs zero tracking history to produce a first answer — which is what makes it work on day one. Today's forecast engine computes a per-goal weekly rate and a date; it never produces the *sequence*, so it can't tell him which goal is blocking which.

Month is the default, but: "we should have all the usual options where I can zoom in to a day or a week."

### The Current Activity view is a Pomodoro timer (pp. 73–77)
> "The 'Current Activity' view is basically a **powerful Pomodoro timer** that integrates with the Zen Routine framework. It aims to help you focus while you're working on the task at hand, while at the same time linking with the scheduling and tracking features."

The mock carries, all on one screen: a large circular countdown (`02:36`), a LIVE/IDLE state badge, `4:22 / 8hrs` ("time tracked so far" / "total estimated time" — **lifetime against the goal, not today**), a row of five tomatoes ("completed and 'available' pomodoros in this session"), a notes icon ("hit this to add notes to this activity"), and a full-day timeline ribbon 7am→11pm with a green "you are here (in time)" marker.

### Scheduled ≠ started ≠ tracked (p. 77)
> "**By default activities 'start' when scheduled but do not 'track'.** The user has to actively indicate that the app can start tracking. The user can also pause tracking. In the use case below the user started the activity some time after it was scheduled and later had to pause."

The IDLE mock shows the shortfall as **grey wedges in the pie, labelled "time spent not tracking"**. Adherence is visible as a gap, not as a number.

### Day Overview (p. 74)
> "The Day Overview shows what goals you're scheduled to be working towards today. It also contains the usual 'Routine View' at the bottom. **Past goals are greyed out while the current goal is more visible.**"

Rows are `time-range · goal name · (hrs tracked toward goal / estimated total)` — e.g. `10.15–12.45 Integrate Analytics Framework (2.5/8hrs)` and later the same day `14.30–17.00 Integrate Analytics Framework (5/8hrs)`. The row names the **goal**, never the activity type.

> "this timer is just not showing seconds, which is kind of silly" — 09:22

> [after linking a session to "Learn guitar"] "and then it says Personal Development instead of Learn Guitar. So it feels like it's **missed the point** a little bit." — 11:25

### The notification drumbeat — a deliberate departure from the design
> "this thing at the end of this document — I don't really like the whole 'start tracking / change of plan'. I'm worried the app would get quite stressful if it's constantly going 'right, next thing, next thing, next thing' — if it's too much of a drumbeat.
> What we could do is: when you load the app, there's a **queue of things that happened before**, and you can quickly go 'yep, yep, yep, did that, did that' — or 'did not do it'. Maybe I can quickly catch up with the past, as opposed to live tracking and update.
> For some people, yes, live tracking and live drumbeat will be useful. But for most people, just a catch-up with 'did you do the things you said you were going to do' — and **if you deviated, by how much**. That could be a very soft thing where you tap the notification and just drag a slider left or right to say 'yeah, kind of did it, but maybe not fully'. And that would be enough data to feed the algorithm." — 21:27

This overrides p. 78–80 of the design. Retroactive catch-up becomes the default mode; live tracking becomes a preference. It also gives issue #13 (measure realtime vs retroactive separately) its real shape: the slider is the retroactive signal, and it is *deliberately* lower-fidelity.

### AI, when it comes
> "in an ideal world, if we have AI features, I could just ask 'when is the Facebook login feature due to be complete?' and it says 'Friday 14th of September', and I go 'no, that's not good, I need to jump into my goals and push that further up'. Or, if we're using an MCP integration, tell it 'push that right to the top', or 'I need that done on Wednesday, can you move things around?' — but for now let's assume no AI integration." — 28:33

Note the shape: the AI's *whole job* is reading and rewriting the goal ordering and the routine. Which is another reason ordering has to be the priority model — it is the thing an agent can manipulate.

## Part 3 — Verified defects

Each of these was reproduced in the recording and traced to a line on `main`.

### D1 · Onboarding shows slide 1 five times — `p0`
`src/screens/OnboardingScreen.tsx` defines five distinct slides (lines 25–61). In the recording, at 14s, 16s, 18s, 20s, 22s and 24s the screen shows slide 1's emoji, title and description every time — while the button flips to "Get Started" at 24s, proving `currentIndex` **did** reach 4. The internal index advances; the viewport never moves.

Cause: `handleNext` (line 73) calls `flatListRef.current?.scrollToIndex()` on a horizontal `FlatList` with **no `getItemLayout`**, which does not scroll on react-native-web. Compounding it, `SCREEN_WIDTH` is read from `Dimensions.get('window')` at module scope (line 15), so the slide width is the *window* width, fixed at load, and wrong after any resize.

> "I click next three times but nothing changed, and then all of a sudden I was dropped in the app." — 00:28

This is the first thing every new user sees, on the only surface that currently exists.

### D2 · "Link to Goal" shows raw minutes and mislabels them
`src/components/tracking/QuickStart.tsx:193` renders `{goal.loggedMinutes} / {goal.estimatedMinutes} min this week`, bypassing `formatDuration`. Observed: **"240 / 1200 min this week"**.

> "these should be converted to hours once it's over 60 minutes" — 11:14

The worse half he didn't catch: those are **lifetime** totals, not weekly. The same goal's card reads `4h / 20h` — the identical numbers. The label is wrong, not just the units.

### D3 · The active timer never shows seconds
`src/components/tracking/ActiveTimer.tsx` tracks `elapsedMinutes` and renders `formatDuration(elapsedMinutes)`, which returns `0m` below one minute. Observed: a started timer reading `0:00` for a full minute. A seconds-capable renderer exists lower in the same file (lines 185–237) and is not the one in use.

### D4 · Goals filter chips render as tall columns on web
Both filter rows on the Goals screen (`All 3 / Active 3 / Completed 0 / Paused 0 / Archived 0`, and `All types / Work / Side Project / …`) render as ~265px-tall vertical bars with the label floating mid-height.

> "this feels like broken CSS or something" — 16:27 · "whoa, this is quite broken, this view" — 14:31

A horizontal `ScrollView` in a column parent stretches to fill the cross axis on RNW; the chips need `alignItems: 'flex-start'` on the content container (or an explicit chip height).

### D5 · The week starts on Sunday
`DayOfWeek` is documented `0 = Sunday` (`src/core/types/RoutineBlock.ts:4`), the day-name arrays start at Sunday (`src/core/utils/time.ts:62`, `CalendarScreen.tsx:421`), and validation says so (`validation.ts:49`).

> "my week I would start on a Monday and end on a Sunday, as opposed to Sunday to Monday. There's no setting to change that; it would be nice to add it to the settings." — 12:11

The design's week strip is `M T W T F S S`. Monday-first should be the **default**, with the setting as the escape hatch.

### D6 · The time picker is a full-screen modal
> "this is a crazy widget — why is it not embedded? I should just have a little drop-down here." — 14:05

`src/components/routine/TimePicker.tsx` takes over the whole screen to pick one time. Same complaint applies to New Goal:

> "now I have to go all the way to the top right. We don't need the whole page — I was expecting little pop-ups and things." — 16:05

### D7 · Goal estimate accepts only bare minutes
> "I'd rather this be able to read, if I do '12 hours' for example — '12h' or '12hr'. I'd rather this input field knows what I'm talking about." — 15:06

Then, live: "6000 minutes… 500… 600… okay, 720 is the number I'm looking for." Four attempts to enter twelve hours.

### D8 · A calendar exists but is the wrong calendar, and is unreachable
`src/screens/CalendarScreen.tsx` exists and is registered on the root stack — reachable **only** via a button on the Analytics screen. It shows *tracking history*. He never found it, and said "the calendar view, which is completely missing" (18:00) — which is correct about the forecast calendar the design specifies. Two separate problems: the forecast calendar doesn't exist, and the one that does is buried.

### D9 · Can't create a goal while building a routine
> "it'd be great if I could just quickly add goals here while I'm creating the routine" — 14:10

The Block Editor's "Link to Goal" section shows "No active goals for this activity type" and offers no way out of that dead end.

## Part 4 — The conceptual correction

At 17:14 he catches the build making a category error, and half-corrects himself mid-sentence:

> "now, actually, no, that's wrong… I don't want to be committing activities to the routine — sorry, *goals* to the routine. I don't want to be committing activity types."

Reading it against the design: **the routine is made of activity types.** Goals attach to an activity type and then *compete for that type's minutes by priority order*. Pinning a specific goal to a specific recurring block ("every Monday lunchtime I work on the combat system") is exactly what he backed away from — it's brittle, and it makes the forecast trivial instead of useful.

Today's `RoutineBlock.goalId` allows the pin, `BlockEditor` invites it, and the forecast engine then treats goal-linked blocks as dedicated capacity. That's a load-bearing design decision made in the wrong direction — and it is why the forecast can't produce his fill-forward sequence.

**This is the one thing I'd want confirmed before any lane touches it**, because it invalidates working code.

## Part 6 — Sequence

**Phase 0 — repair the record (do first, cheaply).** Re-extract the design with the annotations included and replace `ZEN_ROUTINE_SPEC.md`. Every future lane reads that file; while it's wrong, every lane will drift the same way again. This is the highest-leverage hour in the list.

**Phase 1 — the four defects that make a demo embarrassing.** D1 onboarding, D2 minutes/label, D3 seconds, D4 filter chips. Small, independent, verifiable.

**Phase 2 — re-align the model.** Monday-first (D5). Ordering replaces priority enum. Type and estimate become optional. Confirm Part 4, then unpin goals from blocks if confirmed.

**Phase 3 — build the two missing centrepieces.** The fill-forward forecast calendar (month/week/day, filterable, milestone granularity). The Pomodoro Current Activity view with the day ribbon, tomatoes, notes, and LIVE/IDLE with visible untracked time.

**Phase 4 — the catch-up queue,** replacing the drumbeat. This is where #13 (realtime vs retroactive, measured separately) lands.

**Phase 5 — polish, layout, tab-bar placement, phone-first pass.** The ribbon editor belongs here or in Phase 3 depending on how the single-surface decision lands.
